# EXTRACTION PROTOCOL
### A Visual Design Philosophy for EvacZombie

---

## I. The Doctrine of Operational Clarity

Every interface is a command surface — a place where decisions are made under duress and time pressure. The visual language borrows from military operations doctrine: information exists not to decorate, but to enable action. Color signals intent (amber = threat, green = safe corridor, red = lost). Form signals function. The aesthetic operates as if rendered on hardened field equipment — readable at a glance, forgiving of rough hands. Everything non-essential is stripped. What remains must be meticulously crafted to carry the weight of its presence.

## II. The Tension of Suspended Time

Urgency lives in the visual rhythm. The composition carries an implicit countdown — not through literal timers, but through acute visual tension: diagonal weight, compressed margins, asymmetric balance. Elements lean. The eye is pulled. Negative space is never comfortable — it holds breath, not rest. Each painstakingly resolved composition should feel like a moment frozen mid-operation, the instant before the helicopter lifts.

## III. Material Intelligence

The palette draws from the world of tactical operations and emergency systems: slate and anthracite as ground; amber-gold as signal; acid green as life and safety; red-orange as the encroaching threat. Materials suggest laminated maps, phosphorescent field markers, scratched plexiglass overlays. The visual system is the product of deep expertise — every tonal relationship calibrated with the precision of a master craftsman who understands that a wrong color in a command interface costs lives.

## IV. Systemic Geometry

Screens are built from a disciplined grid that expresses hierarchy through scale contrast — not decoration. The large is massive; the small, surgical. Interface components have hard edges and clean corners, like cut aluminum. Information layers with the transparency of overlaid acetate sheets. Icons are reduced to pure silhouette — the geometry carries the meaning, not surface rendering. This systematic visual language rewards sustained observation: what reads as minimal from distance reveals painstaking complexity up close.

## V. The Human Signal

Amid the operational severity, one recurring counterpoint: the human portrait. Character faces carry warmth, expressiveness — a signal that behind the evacuation numbers are actual lives. These portraits are rendered with artistic care against cold system backgrounds, creating a deliberate and expertly tuned emotional contrast. The people are the reason the machine exists. Their presence, rare and weighted, is the soul of the entire visual system.
