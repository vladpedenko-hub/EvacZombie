### 🎮 Core Loop of the original

**Two gameplay layers:**

**1. Strategic layer (meta-map):**
- A city map with districts infected by zombies
- The player chooses which district to tackle this turn
- Infection levels 1-4: at level 4 the district is lost, infection spreads to neighbors
- Every turn adds new infection hotspots — you can't be everywhere in time

**2. Tactical layer (mission):**
- Deploying military squads on the district map
- Goal: evacuate civilians before time runs out
- Zombies move and infect people in real time
- Residents (colored dots) must reach the evacuation zone

**Units (mercenaries):**
- Snipers, infantry, mines, demolition teams, barricades, gas teams (bait), artillery, orbital bombardment
- Units level up through use
- The unit roster is randomized each run

**Key features:**
- Procedural generation of maps and campaigns
- Dynamic difficulty
- Fully destructible environment
- Up to 3 players co-op (split screen)
- Mod support

### 🎯 What players love about the original
- The unique "you can't save everyone" mechanic — tense decisions
- A random event tree makes every run different
- The soundtrack (surf guitar) — memorable character
- Fast sessions + high replayability
- Humor and absurdity in the setting

### 😤 What gets criticized
- Can get monotonous in long sessions
- The difficulty curve is uneven in places
- Limited variety of tactical maps
- No mobile version (an opportunity!)

## 🔍 Detailed breakdown of key competitors

### After Inc. (Ndemic Creations, 2024)
**Why it matters:** The closest competitor in spirit. Made by the creator of Plague Inc. — one of the most successful paid mobile games in history.

- **Core Loop:** Build a settlement → explore ruins → gather resources → expand → manage survivors. Persistent campaign with multiple settlements.
- **Monetization:** $2 on iOS (free on Android) + DLC packs, no consumable IAP. "Buy once, play forever" — positioned as anti-gacha.
- **Performance:** 3.5 million players, #1 on the charts at launch, top-5 paid games in UK/Germany, top-12 in the USA. One of the best mobile releases of 2024 per Gamespot.
- **Strengths:** Strong brand (Plague Inc.), clean monetization, high quality.
- **Weaknesses:** The city builder is slower-paced than AZS, no real action.
- **Lesson for you:** Paid premium ($2-3) can work with quality and marketing. But it's a "huge gamble," in the developer's own words.

---

### Plague Inc. / Rebel Inc. (Ndemic Creations)
**Why they matter:** The benchmark for indie strategy on mobile. Plague Inc. has been a top-10 paid iOS app for 12 years running.

- **Plague Inc. monetization:** Initially paid at $0.99, later freemium. Paid scenarios and modes ($0.99-$2.99 each). No gacha, no energy.
- **Rebel Inc.:** A similar model. Manage an unstable region, keep the rebels from seizing power. Mechanically similar to AZS (two layers: strategic + tactical).
- **Lesson for you:** Rebel Inc. is the most direct template. A strategic map + tactical decisions at the level layer = a working model on mobile.

---

### Plants vs. Zombies 2 (EA)
- **Core Loop:** Tower defense — place plants → repel the zombie wave. Simple and clear.
- **Monetization:** F2P + gacha (plant seeds), energy, premium currency. Aggressive, but it works.
- **Revenue:** A steady $1.1-1.4M a week per Sensor Tower (Q1-Q3 2024).
- **Lesson:** Gacha on "units" (in your case, cards/abilities) is a proven mechanic. You already have a card system — that's good.

---

### State of Survival / Whiteout Survival (FunPlus / Century Games)
- Whiteout Survival: **$1 billion** in 17 months, $100M+ a month at peak.
- **BUT:** A completely different genre (4X base builder with P2W), a target audience of ages 30-50, enormous UA budgets.
- **Lesson for you:** These aren't your direct competitors. Don't try to compete in this segment — it's too expensive.

## 💡 Strategic insights for EvacZombie

### 🟢 Market gap — where there's room

**1. No tactical real-time zombie strategy on mobile**
Every top zombie strategy game is either tower defense (static) or a base builder (slow-paced). Nobody does what AZS does: a fast tactical mission with unit placement and real-time civilian rescue. **This is your niche.**

**2. No tense "you can't save everyone" mechanic**
AZS created unique emotional pressure — you choose who to sacrifice. That's rare on mobile, where everything is usually upbeat.

**3. Midcore with short sessions**
Successful mobile strategy games are either too casual (PvZ) or too heavy (Whiteout). The "smart, fast tactics" niche for a 5-10 minute session is weakly occupied.

### 🎯 Positioning

**Recommended:** "Mini-strategy with tactical action" — not an AZS clone, but a standalone product.

**Reference pitch:** *"Rebel Inc. meets Plants vs. Zombies — real-time tactical decisions, fast sessions, save people from zombies"*

**Target audience:**
- Men 22-35, who enjoy strategy games
- Played PvZ, Into the Dead, After Inc.
- Value depth but don't want to invest hours
- **NOT** a hardcore MOBA/RTS audience

### 💰 Monetization — recommendations

I see in your project: **CardManager, LootboxData, EnergyManager** — these are already F2P mechanics. A good foundation.

**Optimal model for your niche:**

| Mechanic | Recommendation | Rationale |
|----------|-------------|-------------|
| **Energy** | ✅ Keep it, but generous | Retention + monetization. But not greedy — After Inc. showed players value fair play |
| **Card progression** | ✅ Strong choice | Unlocking/upgrading units via cards — clear, replayable |
| **Lootbox** | ⚠️ Careful | Gacha works but causes toxicity. Better: Battle Pass or direct purchase |
| **Battle Pass** | ✅ Recommended | $4.99/month, cosmetics + progression boost. The best LTV tool of 2024-2026 |
| **Seasonal events** | ✅ Mandatory | Retains long-term players |
| **Ads (rewarded)** | ✅ For casual players | Extra revive, x2 reward — an accepted mechanic |

**The model I recommend:**
- Free to play
- Energy (soft — 5 lives, 30-min regen)
- Card-based unit progression (like Clash Royale, but without PvP)
- Seasonal Battle Pass
- Cosmetic skins for units/city
- Rewarded ads for casual players

### ⚠️ Risks and warnings

**1. Controls — the main barrier**
AZS works with a mouse. Placing and controlling units via taps is a nontrivial UX problem. This will be your single most important design challenge.
*Solution:* Simplify — tap to place, drag to reposition, large hit zones. No small elements.

**2. The zombie genre is oversaturated**
Hundreds of zombie games in the App Store. You need clear differentiation in screenshots and the first 10 seconds of gameplay.
*Solution:* The visual style needs to stand out (I see Synty assets — low-poly looks good), focus on the unique evacuation mechanic in UA creatives.

**3. Retention — hard for tactical games**
PvZ holds millions because of endless content and social mechanics. You need either strong progression or procedural generation.
*Solution:* Map randomization + event modes + card meta-progression.

**4. UA Cost — midcore is pricier than casual**
CPI (cost per install) for strategy games: $2-8 in the US. The UA budget is critical.
*Solution:* Start with organic growth via ASO and community (TikTok, Reddit). UA — after finding good retention.

## 📋 Development recommendations (based on the analysis)

**What definitely needs doing:**
1. Solve the tap-to-place control UX — build a prototype and test it with real people
2. Add "story feel" — narrative, characters, emotion (both After Inc. and AZS do this)
3. Implement procedural level generation — retention will die without it
4. Soft launch in Tier-3 countries before the global release (Canada, Australia, Philippines)
5. Measure D1/D7/D30 retention BEFORE active monetization

**What's okay to skip:**
- Multiplayer/PvP at launch — expensive and complex
- AAA-level 3D graphics — Synty low-poly is good enough
- Clan/social features in v1.0
